Beginner's guide

What are AI coins?

“AI coin” is a loose label for a cryptocurrency associated with an artificial-intelligence project or narrative. The label alone tells you little about what the software does or why its token might be needed.

Start with the project, then the token

A project might supply computing resources, coordinate AI services, or use AI in an application. Those are different activities. A token does not automatically give you ownership of an AI model, shares in the developer, or rights to its revenue. Check its specific terms.

Ask two separate questions: what does the product do, and what role does this specific token play? A useful product does not by itself establish a fair token price.

Two examples, not a ranking

  • Bittensor: its documentation describes subnets producing digital services such as compute and inference, with participants rewarded through the network. TAO is its native token.
  • Render Network: its project overview describes distributed GPU rendering and compute, including creative and generative-AI work.

These are project descriptions, not independent endorsements, a complete market list or recommendations to buy. Names, products and token arrangements can change; check current primary documentation.

Questions worth writing down

  • Can I explain the working product without repeating its marketing?
  • What evidence supports the claimed usage?
  • What rights or functions does the token actually provide?
  • What am I still assuming about its future price?
AI branding does not establish investment quality. The SEC, NASAA and FINRA's January 2024 alert warns about misleading AI-related investment promotions. Crypto assets can lose value; a learning exercise cannot remove that risk.
