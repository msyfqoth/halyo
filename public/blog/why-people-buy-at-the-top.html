Pause the rush

Crypto FOMO: why a rising price can feel like a deadline

You see a price rise, then another success story. Waiting starts to feel like missing the only opportunity. That feeling is a reason to pause and check the claim, not evidence about the next price move.

Attention is not a timing signal

The SEC's September 2021 crypto-investment alert describes how fear of missing out can make people less sceptical of a pitch. It does not establish that every popular coin is at its top or that every late buyer will lose.

A market high is identified after the fact. A price can rise further, fall, or stay flat after attracting attention. Buying early also does not guarantee a profitable exit.

Keep the evidence within its limits

BIS Bulletin 69, published in February 2023, reported retail losses on bitcoin holdings using platform data from August 2015 to December 2022. That historical finding is not a current loss rate for AI-coin traders and does not explain every crypto loss.

Try separating three things

  1. Observation: what did I actually see, on which dates and from which source?
  2. Assumption: what am I expecting to continue, and why?
  3. Unknown: what would this chart or promotion still not tell me?

The same habit helps when reviewing a backtest: record the data source and costs, then question how the settings were chosen. A strong result on selected past prices is not a forecast or a way to locate the next top.
