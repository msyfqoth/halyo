Protect yourself

How to spot an AI coin scam before you lose money

The AI hype has attracted brilliant engineers — and a flood of scammers. Because "AI" sounds exciting and complex, it's the perfect cover for taking money from people who don't want to feel left behind.

The good news: almost every scam uses the same handful of tricks. Once you can see them, they're hard to un-see. Here are the red flags.

01Guaranteed or "risk-free" returns

This is the biggest one. No honest person can guarantee a return on any investment — ever. "Guaranteed 10% a day," "risk-free AI trading bot," "can't lose" — all lies, every time. Real investing has no certainties. The moment someone promises certainty, walk away.

02Urgency and FOMO pressure

"Only 24 hours left." "Presale ends tonight." "Last chance before 100x." Urgency exists to stop you thinking. A real project doesn't need a countdown timer to pressure you in. If you feel rushed, that's the scam working — slow down.

03An anonymous or fake team

Who actually built this? Real projects have named founders with real histories you can verify. Scams hide behind anonymous handles, stock-photo "team" pages, or fake advisors who never agreed to be listed. If you can't find out who's behind it, assume the worst.

04Screenshots of huge profits

"Look how much our members made!" Profit screenshots are trivially faked — anyone can edit a number in a browser in seconds. They prove nothing. Be especially wary of "gurus" whose real income is selling you a course or group, not trading.

05"Send crypto, get more back"

Any version of "send 1 coin to this address and get 2 back" is always a theft. No exceptions. Fake giveaways — often impersonating famous people — are one of the most common crypto scams. The moment you send, it's gone.

06A token with no real use

Ask the simple question: what does this actually do? Many AI coins have a slick website, a buzzword-filled whitepaper, and... no working product. If the only reason to buy is "the price will go up," you're not investing — you're hoping someone buys it from you for more. That's the greater-fool game, and someone's always left holding it.

The honest truth: even real AI coins are high-risk and can lose most of their value. Spotting scams keeps you from the obvious traps — but it doesn't make the legitimate ones safe. Both require caution.

The one habit that protects you most

Before anything else: slow down and understand what you're looking at. Scammers rely on speed, excitement and fear of missing out. Someone who calmly asks "who built this, what does it do, and why would the price hold up?" is a terrible target — because the answers to those questions expose almost every scam.
